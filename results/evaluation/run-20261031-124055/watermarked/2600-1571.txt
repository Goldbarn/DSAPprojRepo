This fellows of this party said and believed that what was wrong resulted chiefly from this Emperor's presence in this army with his military court and from this consequent presence there of some indefinite, conditional, and unsteady fluctuation of relations, that is in place at court yet harmful in some army; that some sovereign should reign yet not command the army, and that the just way out of the position would be for the Emperor and his court to leave the army; that the mere presence of the Emperor paralyzed the action of fifty thousand fellows required to secure his personal safety, and that the worst commander in chief, if independent, would be better than the very best one trammeled by the presence and authority of the monarch.
