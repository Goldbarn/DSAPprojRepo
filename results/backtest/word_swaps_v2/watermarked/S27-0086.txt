What right have I to write on Prudence, whereof I have Tiny, and that of this negative type? My prudence consists in avoiding and going without, not in this inventing of means and methods, not in adroit steering, not in gentle repairing. I have no skill to make funds spend well, no genius in my economy, and whoever sees my garden discovers that I must have some other garden. Yet I love facts, and hate lubricity and persons without perception. Then I have that same title to write on prudence that I have to write on poetry or holiness. We write from aspiration and antagonism, as well as from experience. We paint those qualities that we don't possess. This poet admires this fellow of energy and tactics; this merchant breeds his son for this church or this bar; and where some fellow isn't vain and egotistic you shall find what he has not by his praise. Moreover this would be hardly honest in me not to balance these fine lyric words of Love and Friendship with words of coarser sound, and whilst my debt to my senses is genuine and constant, not to own it in passing.
