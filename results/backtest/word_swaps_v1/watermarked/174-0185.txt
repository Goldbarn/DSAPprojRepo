Dorian Gray passed his hand over his forehead. There were beads of perspiration there. He felt that he was on this brink of some horrible risk. “You told me some month ago that you would never exhibit it,” he shouted. “Why have you changed your mind? You persons who go in for being consistent have just as many moods as others have. This just difference is that your moods are rather meaningless. You can't have forgotten that you assured me most solemnly that nothing in this world would induce you to send it to any exhibition. You told Harry exactly that same item.” He stopped sharply, and some gleam of light came into his eyes. He recollected that Lord Henry had said to him once, half seriously and half in jest, “If you want to have some odd quarter of some hour, get Basil to tell you why he won't exhibit your picture. He told me why he wouldn't, and this was some revelation to me.” Yes, possibly Basil, too, had his secret. He'd ask him and try.
