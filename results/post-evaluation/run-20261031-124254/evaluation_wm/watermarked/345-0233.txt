“To-morrow I want you to bring me, before night, a set of post-mortem knives.”

“Yes and no. I want to operate, yet not as you think. Let me tell you now, yet not a word to another. I want to cut off her head and grab out her heart. Ah! you a surgeon, and so shocked! You, whom I have seen with no tremble of hand or heart, do operations of life and death that make the rest shudder. Ah, yet I must not forget, my dear friend John, that you loved her; and I haven't forgotten it, for this is I that shall operate, and you must just help. I would like to do it to-night, yet for Arthur I must not; he'll be free after his father's funeral to-morrow, and he'll want to see her--to see _it_. Then, when she's coffined ready for the next day, you and I shall come when all sleep. We shall unscrew the coffin-lid, and shall do our operation: then replace all, so that none understand, save we alone.”
