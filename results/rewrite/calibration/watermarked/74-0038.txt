“Aunt, you don't whack Sid when he takes it.”

“Well, Sid don't torment a body the way you do. You'd be always into that sugar if I warn't watching you.”

Soon she stepped into her kitchen. Sid, happy at his immunity, reached over for the sugar-bowl with some glorying over Tom that was nearly unbearable. Then Sid's fingers slipped; his bowl dropped, then broke. Tom felt pure ecstasy. So great was that ecstasy that he held his tongue, quite silent. He told himself that he would never speak, even when his aunt came back; he would sit perfectly still till she asked who had done this mischief, then he would tell, and nothing in this world would be so good as seeing that pet model “catch it.” He was so full of exultation that he could hardly hold himself when the old lady came back, stood over the wreck, then let out lightnings of wrath from over her spectacles.
