That idea warn't no slouch; that grindstone warn't no slouch neither; yet we allowed we would tackle her. It warn't quite midnight yet, so we cleared out for that mill, leaving Jim at work. We smouched our grindstone, then set out to roll her home, but that was mighty tough going. Sometimes, do what we could, we couldn't hold her from falling over, and she come mighty near mashing us every time. Tom said she was going to get one of us, sure, before we done. We had her half way when we was plumb done, most drownded in sweat. We could see that warn't no use; we must get Jim. So he raised up his bed, slid his chain off of that bed-leg, wrapt that chain round his neck, then we crawled out through our hole, down there, where Jim and me set into that grindstone, walked her along like nothing; Tom superintended. He could out-superintend any boy I ever see. He knowed how to do everything.
