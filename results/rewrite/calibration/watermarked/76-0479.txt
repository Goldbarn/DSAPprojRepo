That idea warn't no slouch; that grindstone warn't no slouch neither; yet we allowed we'd tackle her. It warn't quite midnight yet, so we cleared out for that mill, leaving Jim at work. We smouched our grindstone, then set out to roll her home, but that was a mighty tough job. Sometimes, do what we could, we couldn't keep her from falling over, then she come mighty near mashing us every time. Tom said she was going to get one of us, sure, before we done. We rolled her half way; then we was plumb played out, most drownded in sweat. We see that warn't no use; we must get Jim. So Jim raised up his bed, slid his chain off of that bed-leg, wrapt that chain round his neck, then we crawled out through our hole, down there, then Jim and me laid into that grindstone, walked her along like nothing; Tom superintended. He could out-superintend any boy I ever see. He could do everything.
