P.P.S.--Hawkins may stay one night with his mother. J. T.

You can fancy how that letter excited me. I was half beside myself with glee; if ever I despised a man, it was old Tom Redruth, whose only occupation was grumbling and lamenting. Any under-gamekeeper would gladly have taken his place; yet the squire's pleasure was otherwise, and his pleasure passed for law among them all. Only old Redruth would have dared even to grumble.

Next morning I set out with him on foot for the Admiral Benbow, where I found my mother well and cheerful. Our captain, after causing so much discomfort for so long, was gone where the wicked cease from troubling. At the squire's orders everything was repaired; the public rooms and sign were repainted. He had added some furniture, especially a beautiful armchair for mother in the bar. He had also found her an apprentice boy, so that she would have help during my absence.
