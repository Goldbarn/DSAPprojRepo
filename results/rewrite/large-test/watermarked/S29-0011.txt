To call the commons a check upon the king presupposes two things:

First.--That without supervision the king cannot be trusted; in other words, that monarchy naturally suffers from a thirst for absolute power.

Secondly.--That appointment to this office implies greater wisdom in the commons, or greater desert of trust, than in the crown.

Yet this same constitution allows the commons to check the king by withholding supplies, then allows the king to check the commons by rejecting their other bills. Thus it assumes greater wisdom in the king than in those already assumed wiser than himself. A mere absurdity!
