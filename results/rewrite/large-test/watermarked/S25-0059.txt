Observe that familiar instances are more evident than the general principle itself. Thus the law of contradiction denies that anything can possess a certain property while also lacking it. Once understood, this is obvious; yet less obvious than that a rose before our eyes cannot be red and not red. (Of course, parts of a rose may be red while others are not; or its pink may leave us uncertain whether to call it red. In the former case, plainly the whole rose is not red; in the latter, settling a precise definition of 'red' makes the answer theoretically definite.) Usually we come to understand the general principle through particular instances. Without examples, only those accustomed to abstractions can readily comprehend a general principle.
