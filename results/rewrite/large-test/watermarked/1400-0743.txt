Never had I breathed, and never would I breathe, or so I resolved, a word of Estella to Provis. But I said to Herbert that, before I could go abroad, I must see both Estella and Miss Havisham. This was when we were left alone on the night of the day when Provis told us his story. I resolved to go out to Richmond next day, and I went.

Upon my arrival at Mrs. Brandley's, Estella's maid came at a summons to tell me that Estella had gone into the country. Where? To Satis House, as usual. Not as usual, I said, for she had never yet gone there without me; when would she come back? Her maid's answer, given with an air of reservation that deepened my perplexity, was that she believed her return would be for a little while only. I could make nothing of this, except that it was meant that I should make nothing of it, and I went home again in complete discomfiture.
