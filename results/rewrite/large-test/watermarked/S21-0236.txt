Following are Mrs. Trollope’s emotions. She writes a few months later in the same year, 1827, coming in at the mouth of the Mississippi:

‘Our first indication of approaching land was this mighty river’s muddy mass of waters pouring forth and mingling with the deep blue of the Mexican Gulf. Never have I seen a scene so utterly desolate as this entry to the Mississippi. Had Dante seen it, he might have drawn images of another Borgia from its horrors. One solitary object rises above the eddying waters: the mast of a vessel long since wrecked while attempting to cross the bar. It still stands, a dismal witness of past destruction and a boding prophet of destruction to come.’
