“Thank you, sir,” said Poole, something like triumph sounding in his voice. Candle in hand, he guided Mr. Utterson back across the yard into the great kitchen. Its fire was out; beetles were leaping on its floor.

“Sir,” he said, looking Mr. Utterson in the eyes, “Was that my master’s voice?”

“It seems much altered,” replied the lawyer, very pale, giving look for look.

“Changed? Well, yes, I think so,” said the butler. “Have I served this man twenty years in his house, to be deceived about his voice? No, sir; master’s murdered; murdered eight days ago, when we heard him cry out upon God’s name; and _who’s_ in there instead of him, and _why_ it stays there, is a thing that cries to Heaven, Mr. Utterson!”
