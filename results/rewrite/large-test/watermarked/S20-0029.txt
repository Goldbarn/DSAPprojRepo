Attempting to estimate structural difference amongst allied domestic races soon involves us in doubt, since their descent from one parent species or several remains unknown. Clearing up this point would be interesting. If, for instance, greyhound, bloodhound, terrier, spaniel and bull-dog, which all propagate their kind truly, could be shown to have sprung from a single species, such facts would give us great reason to doubt the immutability of many closely allied natural species, such as the many foxes inhabiting different quarters of the world. I do not believe, as we shall presently see, that domestication has produced the whole difference amongst the several breeds of dog; I believe that descent from distinct species accounts for a small fraction. Strongly marked races of some other domesticated species offer presumptive or even strong evidence of a single wild ancestry.
