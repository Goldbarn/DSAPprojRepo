She felt the compliment paid to herself and her sister. It soothed her, yet could give her no consolation for the contempt thus drawn upon the rest of her family by themselves. She considered that Jane’s disappointment had, indeed, sprung from her nearest relations; she reflected that such impropriety must seriously injure both sisters’ credit, and felt depressed beyond anything she had ever known before.

For two hours she wandered along the lane, giving way to every variety of thought, considering events anew, weighing probabilities, and reconciling herself, as well as she could, to so sudden and important a change. At last fatigue, together with the remembrance of her long absence, made her go home. She entered the house wishing to appear cheerful as usual, resolved to suppress such reflections as must make her unfit for conversation.
