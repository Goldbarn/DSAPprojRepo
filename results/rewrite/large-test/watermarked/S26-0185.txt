Sometimes Fronto leaves eloquence's heights for practical advice, suggesting, for instance, how Marcus should manage his suite. He admits that maintaining harmony among courtiers is harder than taming lions with a lute; success requires jealousy's eradication. 'Do not let your friends,' says Fronto,'[30] 'envy each other, or think that what you give to another is filched from them.

Keep away envy from your suite, and you will find your friends kindly and harmonious.'

Occasional allusions reveal his daily life; we could wish them more frequent. He visits the theatre or law-courts,[31] takes part in court ceremony; yet his heart stays with his books.
