The men of this party said and thought that our troubles came chiefly from his Majesty's presence with his military court at the front, and from that consequent presence of an indefinite, conditional, unsteady fluctuation of relations, which is in place at court yet harmful to troops; that a sovereign should reign, not command, and that our only way out of this position would be for him and his court to quit his army; that his mere presence paralyzed fifty thousand men required to secure his personal safety, and that the worst commander in chief, if independent, would be better than the very best one trammeled by his presence and authority.
