While the young fellow came to his place on that step, Rostopchín stood frowning, rubbing his face with his hand.

“Lads!” said he, his voice having some metallic ring. “This fellow, Vereshchágin, is that scoundrel through whose doing Moscow is perishing.”

That young fellow in his fur-lined coat, stooping a little, stood submissive, fingers clasped before him. His emaciated young face, disfigured by his half-shaven head, hung without hope. At the count's first words he slowly lifted that face, then looked at him as though he wanted to say something or at least meet his eye. Yet Rostopchín did not look at him. Upon that fellow's thin neck one vein swelled like a cord, showing blue behind his ear, then all at once his face flushed.
