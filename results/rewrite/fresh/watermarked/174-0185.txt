Dorian Gray drew his hand over his forehead, wet with beads of perspiration. He felt that he stood upon the very brink of some horrible danger. “You told me a month ago that you would never exhibit it,” he cried. “What has made you change your mind? You people that set out to be consistent have just as many moods as anyone else. All that differs is that your moods are rather meaningless. You could not have forgotten that you assured me, very solemnly, that nothing would ever induce you to send this to any exhibition. You told Harry that same thing.” He stopped short, then some light came into his eyes. He recalled that Lord Henry had said to him once, half seriously, half in jest, “If you want to have a strange quarter of an hour, get Basil to tell you why he won't exhibit your picture. He told me why he wouldn't, and that was a revelation to me.” Yes, Basil, too, might have his secret. He would ask him, and try.
