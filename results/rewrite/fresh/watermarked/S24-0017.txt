Sect. 50. Yet since gold and silver, being of little use to the life of man in proportion to food, raiment, and carriage, has its value only through the consent of men, whereof labour yet makes, in great part, its measure, it is plain that men have agreed upon a disproportionate, unequal possession of this earth, they having, through a tacit, voluntary consent, discovered a way that a man may fairly possess more land than he himself could make use of, through receiving in exchange for that overplus gold and silver, which may be hoarded without injury to any one; these metals never spoiling or decaying in their possessor's hands. This partage of things in an inequality of private possessions, men have made practicable out of the bounds of society, without compact, only through putting some value on gold and silver, tacitly agreeing in the use of money: since in governments, laws regulate the right of property, and the possession of land is determined by positive constitutions.
