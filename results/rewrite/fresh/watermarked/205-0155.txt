That night was like travelling into some far country, such as I had never expected to see. I seemed never before to have heard the town-clock strike, or the evening sounds of this village; since we slept with our windows open, behind that grating. I could see my native village in the light of the Middle Ages, our Concord turned into a Rhine stream, with visions of knights, of castles, passing before me. Those were voices of old burghers that I heard upon the streets. I was an involuntary spectator, an auditor of whatever was done or said in the kitchen of that adjacent village-inn—a wholly new, rare experience to me. That was a closer view of my native town. I was fairly inside of it. I never had seen its institutions before. This is one of its peculiar institutions; for this is a shire town. I began to understand what its inhabitants were about.
