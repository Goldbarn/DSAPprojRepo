This evil is not one that exists only in theory; you may expect me therefore to set out those cases where the public of this age and country improperly invests its own preferences with the character of moral law. I am not writing some essay upon aberrations of existing moral feeling. That is too weighty a subject to be discussed parenthetically, by way of illustration. Yet examples must be given, to show that my principle is of serious, practical moment, and that I am not endeavouring to erect a barrier against imaginary evils. Many instances make it easy to show that to extend what may be called moral police, till it encroaches upon the most unquestionably legitimate liberty of the individual, is one of the most universal of human propensities.
