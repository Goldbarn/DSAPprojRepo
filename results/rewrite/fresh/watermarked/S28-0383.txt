To the second, that is, to the pretended establishment of the common and state law by the Constitution, I answer, that they are expressly made subject “to such alterations and provisions as the legislature shall from time to time make concerning the same.” They are therefore at any moment liable to repeal through ordinary legislative power, and of course have no constitutional sanction. That declaration served only to recognize that ancient law, and to remove doubts that might have been occasioned through the Revolution. This could therefore never be considered part of some declaration of rights, which under our constitutions must be intended as limitations upon the power of the government itself.
