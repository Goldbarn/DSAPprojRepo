No large country, we must observe, ever did or could subsist without manufactures of some kind being carried on within it; and whenever any such country is said to have no manufactures, that must be understood of those finer, more improved, or of those fit for distant sale. In every large country both clothing and household furniture, for much the greater part of its people, are the produce of their own industry. This is even more true of those poor countries commonly said to have no manufactures than of those rich countries said to abound in them. In these last you would generally find, in both clothes and household furniture of their lowest rank of people, a much greater proportion of foreign productions than in those former.
