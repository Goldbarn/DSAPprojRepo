What right have I to write on Prudence, whereof I have Little, and that of the negative sort? My prudence consists of avoiding, of going without, not of inventing means and methods, not of adroit steering, not of gentle repairing. I have no skill to make money spend well, no genius for economy, and whoever sees my garden discovers that I must have some other garden. Yet I love facts, and hate lubricity and people without perception. Then I have that same title to write on prudence that I have to write on poetry or holiness. We write out of aspiration and antagonism, as well as out of experience. We paint those qualities we do not possess. The poet admires that man of energy and tactics; the merchant breeds his son for church or bar; and where a man is not vain and egotistic you shall find what he has not by his praise. Moreover it would be hardly honest of me not to balance these fine lyric words of Love and Friendship with words of coarser sound, and whilst my debt to my senses is real and constant, not to own it in passing.
